NDIS Social Media Ads

Reach families and coordinators before they start searching.

Why it matters

Google Ads catches people who are already looking

Social media ads reach people before they get there. A parent scrolling Facebook who sees a genuine story from your team. A coordinator on Instagram who sees how your support workers actually spend their day. A family member who was not actively searching but recognises something in your ad that matches what they need.

I run Meta campaigns, Facebook and Instagram, for NDIS providers. The job is different to Google. You are not answering a search, you are earning attention, so the creative, the message and the targeting all have to work harder.

The work

What I actually build

01

Real photos and real stories outperform everything else

Stock photos of smiling strangers do not work for NDIS advertising. Families scroll past them because they look like every other provider. What stops the scroll is something real: a support worker and a participant at the beach, a team member explaining what they actually do, a short video of a real day.

I help you capture and produce creative that feels genuine. Phone-camera footage, real locations, real people. I coach your team on how to get these moments without a production crew. The best performing NDIS ads I have run were filmed on an iPhone in someone’s backyard.

02

Your ads reach people in your service area, not the whole country

I target by location, age, interests and behaviours. For participant-facing campaigns that means families in your service area with interests related to disability support, the NDIS, or the specific conditions your team supports. For referrer-facing campaigns it means professionals in allied health, support coordination and hospital social work.

Targeting is set tight from the start. I do not run broad campaigns and hope the algorithm works it out. Your budget is limited, and every dollar should reach someone who could realistically become an enquiry.

03 · Building trust

Social ads warm people up. The conversion happens later

Unlike Google Ads, where someone clicks and enquires in the same session, social ads usually work across several touches. Someone sees your ad, visits your page, reads a few posts, comes back to your website a week later, and then calls.

So I structure campaigns in layers. Engagement campaigns build familiarity in your target area. Conversion campaigns then target the people who have already engaged. Retargeting reaches people who visited your website without getting in touch.

04 · Lead forms

The lead forms that waste your team’s time

Meta offers lead form campaigns where people submit their details without leaving Facebook. In my experience with NDIS providers, lead form quality is consistently poor at scale. People fill them in without real intent, and your team spends its week chasing contacts who do not remember submitting anything.

I run website conversion campaigns instead. That extra step of clicking through to a landing page filters out low-intent enquiries and delivers people who have read about your service and chosen to get in touch. A smaller number of better enquiries.

05 · What to expect

Social ads build momentum over time

Social advertising is not as immediately responsive as Google search ads. The first month builds the audience, tests creative and gathers data. Results improve as the campaigns learn who engages and who converts.

  1. Week 1 to 2

    Campaign build, creative production, audience setup and tracking.

  2. Month 1

    Engagement campaigns live, building the audience and warming the pixel.

  3. Month 2

    Conversion campaigns layer in, and the first enquiries arrive from the warmed audience.

  4. Month 3 and beyond

    Retargeting performing, creative refreshed, cost per enquiry improving.

Social ads work best as one layer of a broader plan. The families who see your ad and later search for you on Google should find a website that delivers on what the ad promised, which is what SEO and Google Ads are for.

Questions

What providers ask about social media ads

What is the difference between Google Ads and social media ads for NDIS?

Google Ads reach people who are actively searching for a provider right now. Social ads reach people before they search, building familiarity and trust. Google is intent-based: someone types a query and your ad appears. Social is interruption-based: someone is scrolling and your ad earns their attention. Both work, and they do different jobs.

How much should I spend on Facebook ads?

For a local NDIS provider, $1,000 to $2,500 per month in ad spend is a reasonable starting point. Social campaigns need enough budget to build data across the engagement and conversion layers. Below $1,000 per month results are slow to materialise, because the campaigns never get the volume they need to optimise.

Do I need professional photos and video?

No. The best performing creative I have seen from NDIS providers was filmed on a phone. Real footage of real people in real settings outperforms polished production. I coach your team on what to capture and how, then handle the editing and the ad build.

Can I boost posts instead of running proper campaigns?

You can, but boosted posts offer limited targeting and optimisation compared with campaigns run through Ads Manager. Boosting is fine for reach. If the goal is enquiries, a properly structured campaign will deliver more for the same spend.
