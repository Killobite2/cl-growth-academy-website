Why it matters

They will look you up before they call

A family considering your provider will look at your social media before they call. A coordinator deciding between two referral options will check your online presence. What they find there decides whether they pick up the phone or keep scrolling.

This page covers both halves: the organic content that builds trust over time, and the paid campaigns that put you in front of people before they have started searching. They do different jobs, and they work better together.

Organic

Content that shows rather than tells

01

Your team is your best content

The strongest content an NDIS provider can publish is the real work, captured simply. A support worker and a participant at the park. A team member explaining what they love about their job. A photo from a real day, not a staged one.

We help you build a content calendar around your team and your participants, with consent. We coach your staff on how to capture genuine moments on a phone camera: what to look for, how to frame it, and how to get a usable photo or ten-second clip without turning it into a production.

The content you produce this way outperforms anything a marketing agency could produce from the outside. Because it is real, and people can tell.

02

Different platforms, different audiences

LinkedIn, Facebook and Instagram each serve a different audience for NDIS providers. Posting the same content to all three is common, and it underperforms on all three.

Facebook and Instagram are where families are. Content here should be participant-facing: stories, day-in-the-life posts, team introductions, and information about services and how to access them. The tone is warm, direct and personal.

LinkedIn is where coordinators, allied health professionals and referral partners are. Content here is business to business: sector insights, vacancy updates, team capability, and thought leadership from the founder. The tone is professional but still human.

03 · Consistency

Three posts a week for six months beats one viral post

Social media for NDIS providers is a trust-building channel. It works through consistency, not through chasing reach. A provider that posts three times a week, sharing real stories and useful information, builds more trust over six months than one that posts occasionally and hopes for a breakout moment.

We build a posting schedule your team can sustain: a realistic content calendar, a library of reusable content formats, and a process that does not require your founder to spend three hours a week writing captions.

04 · Compounding

Good content feeds the other channels

Content created for social media does not live only on social media. A well-written post about what SIL looks like in your service can become a page on your website that ranks in Google. A video introducing your team can sit on a landing page and improve conversion rates. A story shared on LinkedIn can reach a coordinator who later refers a family.

We plan content with these connections in mind. Every piece of content should do more than one job.

Paid

Reach families and coordinators before they start searching

Google Ads catches people who are already looking. Social ads reach people before they get there. You are not answering a search, you are earning attention, so the creative, the message and the targeting all need to work harder.

05 · Creative

Real photos and real stories outperform everything else

Stock photos of smiling strangers do not work for NDIS advertising. Families scroll past them because they look like every other provider. What stops the scroll is something real: a support worker and a participant at the beach, a team member explaining what they actually do, a short video of a real day.

We help you capture and produce creative that feels genuine. Phone-camera footage, real locations, real people. The best performing NDIS ads we have run were filmed on a phone in someone's backyard.

06 · Targeting and layers

Your budget reaches people in your service area

We target by location, age, interests and behaviours. For participant-facing campaigns that means families in your service area. For referrer-facing campaigns it means professionals in allied health, support coordination and hospital social work.

Campaigns run in layers. Engagement campaigns build familiarity and gather data. Conversion campaigns then target people who have already engaged. Retargeting reaches people who visited your website but did not contact you. Your budget builds a pipeline rather than chasing one-shot conversions.

One thing we avoid: Meta lead form campaigns. In our experience with NDIS providers, lead form quality is consistently poor at scale, and your team spends time chasing contacts who do not remember submitting the form. We run website conversion campaigns instead, which deliver a smaller number of better enquiries.

What to expect

Paid social builds momentum over time

Social advertising is not as immediately responsive as search ads. The first month is about building the audience, testing creative and gathering data.

  1. Week 1 to 2

    Campaign build, creative production, audience setup, tracking.

  2. Month 1

    Engagement campaigns live, building the audience and warming the pixel.

  3. Month 2

    Conversion campaigns layer in, with first enquiries from the warmed audience.

  4. Month 3 and beyond

    Retargeting performing, creative refreshed, cost per enquiry improving.

Organic reach has limits, and a following that never enquires is not worth the effort it takes to build. Social works best alongside search visibility and referral relationships. It is a supporting channel, and a powerful one.

Questions

What providers ask about social media

How often should an NDIS provider post?

Three times a week is a good target for Facebook and Instagram. Two to three times a week on LinkedIn. The specific number matters less than consistency. Posting every day for a month and then going silent for six weeks does more harm than posting three times a week, every week.

Do I need to show participants in my content?

Participant content is the most powerful content a provider can share, but only with proper consent. We help you set up a simple consent process and work within whatever your compliance team requires. If participant content is not possible, staff stories, team introductions and behind-the-scenes content still outperform stock photos.

Do I need professional photos and video?

No. The best performing creative we have seen from NDIS providers was filmed on a phone. Real footage of real people in real settings outperforms polished production. We coach your team on what to capture and how, and we handle the editing and ad build.

How much should I spend on paid social?

For a local NDIS provider, $1,000 to $2,500 per month in ad spend is a reasonable starting point. Social campaigns need enough budget to build data across the engagement and conversion layers. Below $1,000 per month, results are slow to materialise because the campaigns do not get enough volume to optimise.

Should I be on TikTok?

For NDIS providers, probably not yet. The audience is younger than most participant families and coordinators. Facebook, Instagram and LinkedIn cover the three audiences that matter: families, participants and referral partners. If that changes, we will recommend it.

Can my team manage social media themselves?

Yes, and for some providers that is the right approach. We offer a coaching model where we build the strategy, content calendar and templates, and your team handles the posting. We review and refine monthly. This works well for providers with an admin or marketing person who has time but needs direction.
