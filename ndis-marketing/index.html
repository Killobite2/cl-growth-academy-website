Why it matters

Marketing an NDIS provider is a different job

Different to marketing a restaurant, a tradie or an ecommerce store. Four things change the work, and a generic playbook gets all four wrong.

01 · The audience is split

Two audiences, one campaign

Participants and their families find you one way. Support coordinators and allied health professionals find you another. A campaign that talks to both the same way reaches neither well.

02 · Compliance

What you can say, and how you can say it

What you can claim about NDIS funding, what imagery you can use, how you position your services. Get it wrong and you look like every other provider. Get it badly wrong and you have a regulatory problem.

03 · Geography

A provider in Penrith is not competing with one in Geelong

Budgets need to target the areas you actually serve, down to the suburb. Spraying across a state and hoping is how a small budget disappears without a single enquiry.

04 · The moment

People searching are often having a hard week

A plan review that did not go well. A provider relationship that stopped working. A family member trying to work out what is even available. The marketing has to meet them there, with clarity and respect.

Fifteen years in sales and marketing, the last two focused on NDIS and aged care. Every channel, every campaign and every page starts from that.

How they fit

One channel is a tactic. Two engines together is a system.

Most providers start with one thing. An ad campaign, a website refresh, a social push. That is a fine place to start, but the channels do different jobs and they get stronger when they are connected.

  1. Ads deliver this month

    Paid search puts you in front of people searching this week, which is what matters when the roster has gaps now.

  2. SEO compounds over months

    It takes time, but once your pages rank every click is free, and the asset keeps paying after the ad spend stops.

  3. Email catches the "not yet"

    Your list grows from the people who visited but were not ready, and stays in front of families and coordinators until the timing changes.

  4. Referrals close fastest

    A recommendation from a coordinator or an OT arrives pre-trusted, which is why it converts better than anything you can buy.

Run together and each one makes the others stronger. Your ad traffic lands on an optimised page. Your social content reinforces the message someone saw in the ad. Your enquiry process turns all of it into signed agreements instead of missed calls. I run all seven, reported together, so you can see which is pulling its weight and which needs adjusting.

Questions

What providers ask about NDIS marketing

How much should an NDIS provider spend on marketing?

It depends on the service, the geography, and how fast you want to move. A provider offering SIL in one region has a different budget profile to a provider offering community access across a metro area. As a starting point, enough to test one paid channel properly for three months, which for a local provider is typically $1,500 to $3,000 per month in ad spend plus management. If your budget is too low to get a meaningful read, I will tell you.

Do NDIS providers actually need SEO?

If you want families and coordinators to find you when they search, yes. "NDIS provider" plus a suburb, and "SIL vacancy" plus a suburb, are real searches with real intent behind them. If your website does not show up, someone else's does. SEO takes time to build, but the traffic it generates is free and compounds over months.

What marketing channels work best for NDIS providers?

There is no single best channel. Google Ads reach people who are actively searching right now. SEO builds visibility over time. Social builds trust before someone is ready to enquire. Email keeps you in front of leads who need more time. Referral partnerships often convert at the highest rate of all, with zero ad spend. The right mix depends on your services, your region, and where your current enquiries already come from.

Can I just do social media and skip the rest?

You can. But organic social reach is limited, and a following that never enquires is not worth much on its own. Social works best as one layer alongside search visibility and referral relationships. If budget is tight, I would usually start with one paid channel, ads or SEO, and add social once there is a foundation to build on.

How long before I see results?

It depends on the channel. Ads can put you in front of people searching this week. SEO typically takes three to six months to build meaningful rankings. Email and social are relationship channels that compound over time. I set expectations for each channel at the start and report honestly on progress each month.

Will you write the content for my website?

Yes. Every landing page, every ad, every email sequence. Written specifically for NDIS, in plain language, with real stories from your team wherever possible.
