Virtual assistant (offshore talent) vs AI for NDIS providers: which should you choose?

Choose by task. AI suits repeatable drafting and summarising. A virtual assistant (VA) suits work that needs a person on the phone or real judgement. My favourite setup for an NDIS provider is both: a VA who uses AI tools trained on your business, with someone senior checking the output.

Last updated: 20 October 2026 By Chris Lapa, Founder, CL Growth Academy 8 min read

I get asked "VA or AI?" a lot right now. My answer, from a call earlier this year: "I don't believe it's an either or. I think it's just depending on what the task is and the proficiency of the business and a whole bunch of things. I'd rather see a really sexy hybrid of both."

01 · The difference

What is the difference between a virtual assistant and AI?

A virtual assistant is a person, usually working remotely, who takes on tasks you hand over. AI is software, such as ChatGPT, Claude or Grok Bot, that drafts and summarises information when someone gives it instructions.

A VA needs training and management. AI needs setup, clear instructions and a person checking what it produces. Both need someone senior to decide what good looks like.

The bigger difference for an NDIS business is people. Families, participants and support coordinators still want to talk to a person. As I've said more than once, "People still want to speak to people." AI does the admin, and people do the relationships.

02 · Task by task

Which NDIS tasks suit a VA, AI or both?

Here is how I'd split the common tasks in an NDIS business.

Task Best fit Why
Inbox triage Both AI sorts and drafts replies, the VA sends and escalates
Enquiry acknowledgement calls VA Families and coordinators want a person on the first call
Intake follow-up Both AI drafts the follow-up, the VA makes the call and updates the CRM
Rostering VA, with software Rostering software does the matching, a person handles the changes and the phone calls
NDIS claiming VA Needs care with each claim and someone senior approving
Progress notes Both AI can tidy a support worker's notes in an approved tool, the worker checks and signs off
Incident reports Person Keep with your own team, AI stays out of it
Referrer follow-up VA Relationships with coordinators and allied health need a person
Social content calendar Both AI drafts from your brand rules, the VA schedules, a senior person reviews
Monthly newsletter Both Same as social
Meeting notes and summaries AI Fast and repeatable, a person checks the actions
Writing procedures AI Record a task on video, AI turns it into a written procedure
Monthly reporting Both AI pulls and summarises the numbers, a person explains them

The pattern: AI writes the first draft, and a person makes the calls and signs off.

03 · Why both

Why do I like a combination of both?

A VA on their own is limited by what they know about your business. AI on its own has no judgement and agrees with everything you say. Put them together and each covers the other's gap.

The combination I recommend is a VA backed by AI that already knows the sector's dos and don'ts and your brand rules. That way, when the VA builds a content calendar, they're "not just going to their free ChatGPT and spitting out the same dribble that everyone else is doing."

Some owners would rather speak to a VA once a month than talk to Claude at 11 o'clock at night. As I said on that same call, "it's horses for courses". Pick by the task, and by the person running the business.

04 · My own business

How did I bring AI into my own business?

The first thing I did to bring AI into my business was bring in offshore talent. I got everything out of my head with a person first, through walk-and-talk calls and a Loom for every task. The plan is three months side by side on my inbox and calendar, then a review of "the threads, the themes, the easy wins, when do we escalate". After that, AI drafts and a person checks.

I'm bullish on AI, and I still want at least one person helping me run the business. A quick call with a team member often beats a slow back-and-forth with an AI that tells you every idea is great.

I've since built an AI routine that pulls every "in future, don't do this" from Slack and email and turns it into a weekly coaching report for me and my offshore team.

05 · Cost

What does each cost an NDIS business?

AI tools cost a monthly subscription per person, plus the time to set them up. At one business I work with, two assistants were each paying about US$20 to 25 a month out of their own pockets for the AI they needed for the job. Put everyone on one company plan instead, and keep the prompts and files in accounts the business owns.

A VA costs an hourly or monthly rate, plus your time training them.

Size the AI to the business. An off-the-shelf tool with a good set of instructions gets a small provider roughly 80% of the way. A custom build only makes sense once that runs out. When we look at a provider's systems, "if an existing system already does the job and has done the compliance work, we'll tell you that instead of building something." That's part of how we help.

06 · Participant data

Is it safe to use AI with participant information?

Only with rules in place first. The Privacy Act covers NDIS providers whatever their size, because the OAIC (opens in a new tab) says it applies to any health service provider "regardless of turnover". Putting participant information into an AI tool is a use of that information, and possibly a disclosure.

My rules for providers:

  • Use a paid company account, never a free personal one.
  • Keep participant information out of general AI tools unless your privacy policy and consent cover it.
  • Put a person's approval on every output that reaches a participant, family or referrer.
  • Write down which tools are approved and who can use them.

A VA using AI follows the same rules. The VA post covers access and screening for VAs.

07 · Where to start

Where should an NDIS business start with AI?

Start with one manual process and do it the old-fashioned way first. "Once you do it the 'old fashioned way' then you can level up and automate it a lot more." Running it by hand shows you the friction points, where a person has to stay in the loop and where the reputation risk sits.

The National AI Centre's SME survey found 44% of Australian SMEs were using AI in February 2026, and 19% said they don't know how to use it in their business. Health, education and services lead, with more than half of businesses in those sectors using AI (National AI Centre (opens in a new tab)).

Before I bring AI into any process, I check four things:

  1. Is there a manual process clearly costing time or money?
  2. Does it repeat in a pattern?
  3. Is a person willing to own the approval?
  4. Will the outcome be measurably better, or just cool?

Any no, and AI waits. If all four are yes, pick one process, put an approval step on every output, and measure it for 30 to 60 days.

Treat a new AI tool like a new junior. A peer showed me an AI agent that had built a full ad campaign on its own, on the wrong objective and spending on the wrong audience. "It's like giving the new kid the keys to do something when they haven't even learned the basic stuff." Supervise it for the first three months.

Sorting the admin first gives your marketing room to work, and it comes up with most providers I meet as an NDIS marketing agency.

Questions

FAQ

Can AI replace a virtual assistant in an NDIS business?

For some tasks, yes: meeting notes, first drafts of emails and social posts, and turning recorded tasks into written procedures. For calls with families, participants and referrers, claiming and anything needing judgement, you still need a person. A VA using AI tools trained on your business does more than either one alone.

Can AI write progress notes?

AI can tidy up a support worker's notes, in a paid tool your privacy policy and consent cover. The support worker who delivered the support has to check the note and sign it off, because they are the one who knows what happened. Never paste participant details into a free or personal AI account.

Is ChatGPT safe for participant information?

Not on a free or personal account. The Privacy Act covers NDIS providers whatever their size, and putting participant information into an AI tool is a use of that information. Use a paid business plan, check its data settings, cover AI use in your privacy policy and consent, and keep identifying details out unless you have that cover.

How much time can AI save on NDIS admin?

It depends on the task, so measure your own. Time one repeatable task by hand for two weeks, such as writing meeting summaries or drafting enquiry follow-ups. Then run it with AI and a person approving each output for 30 to 60 days and compare the hours. Ignore percentages quoted by vendors.

Do I need a VA to run the AI tools?

Not always. A small provider can run an off-the-shelf AI tool themselves for drafting and notes. Once the volume grows, a VA who runs the AI tools and checks the drafts frees up far more of your time. Either way, someone senior decides the rules the AI follows and reviews what goes out.
